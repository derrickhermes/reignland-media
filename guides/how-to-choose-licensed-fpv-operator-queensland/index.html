# How to Choose a Licensed FPV Drone Operator in Queensland

Introduction

FPV drone cinematography is transforming how properties, venues and events are showcased in Queensland. But with the rise in demand comes a growing number of operators—not all of whom hold the required CASA approvals.

This guide walks you through the due diligence process so you can hire with confidence.

Step 1: Understand What Licensing Is Required

In Queensland's regulatory environment (consistent with national CASA rules), commercial FPV operations require:

Mandatory for Commercial FPV Outdoors

- ReOC — Remote Operator's Certificate. Confirms the business is CASA-approved for commercial operations

- EVLOS or BVLOS approval — Specific authorisation to fly FPV outside standard visual line of sight

Highly Recommended for Any Commercial Work

- Public Liability Insurance — minimum $10M coverage

- Site Risk Assessments — documented flight plan for each job

- Professional Equipment — commercial-grade drone, not consumer toy

Step 3: Ask the Two Questions

Before getting a quote, ask:

Question 1

"Can you provide your CASA ReOC number and confirm you hold current EVLOS approval for outdoor commercial FPV operations?"

Expected response: A straightforward yes, with the ReOC number and EVLOS reference.

Red flags: "I'm working on it," "It's just a small drone so it's fine," "I have a spotter," or changing the subject.

Question 2

"Can you provide a certificate of currency for your public liability insurance that specifically covers drone operations?"

Expected response: A PDF certificate showing the policy period, coverage amount, and the operator's business name matching the ReOC.

Red flags: "I'll send it later" (and never does), "The insurance covers everything," vague policy details.

Step 4: Evaluate Their Work

Licensing is table stakes. The next question is quality.

What to look for

- Smooth, stable footage — FPV should show deliberate, controlled movement, not erratic jerking

- Appropriate speed — a venue tour should move at walking pace through interiors, not race

- Proper exposure — the drone camera should handle indoor/outdoor transitions cleanly

- Sound design — if there's audio, it should complement the visuals, not fight them

- Single-take capability — a true FPV fly-through is one continuous take, not a montage of clips

Red flags in portfolio

- Only standard aerials advertised as FPV (FPV has the cockpit feel—you'll know it when you see it)

- No example of your type of property (commercial, industrial, residential)

- Grainy or compressed footage on their showreel

Step 5: Discuss Your Specific Project

Questions to ask during quoting

1. "Can you fly inside this venue/property?"

- Confirm indoor FPV capability and experience

2. "What's the turnaround time?"

- Standard: raw stabilised + graded footage within 5 business days. Rush available on request?

3. "Do you provide a site-specific risk assessment?"

- Professional operators always say yes

4. "Can you deliver 4K vertical for Reels/TikTok?"

- Important for social media deliverables

5. "What's your cancellation policy?"

- Weather cancelations are normal—how are they handled?

Step 6: Queensland Region Considerations

Region Considerations
**Brisbane** CBD has restricted airspace. Operator must know controlled zones. Indoor flights often preferred.
**Sunshine Coast** Coastal wind conditions. Council operating permits helpful (Reignland holds Sunshine Coast Council permit).
**Gold Coast** Restricted airspace near airport. Heliport traffic considerations.
**Fraser Coast** Distance from major operator bases — factor in travel costs.
**Bundaberg** Regional hub. Few licensed FPV operators. Check travel costs.
**Gympie / Mary Valley** Rural operations with fewer restrictions but distance considerations.

Budget Guide for Queensland FPV

FPV venue fly-throughs start at $495. Standard aerial, hybrid, and event work are quoted per job — size, location, and deliverables affect the price, so always request an itemised quote rather than expecting a fixed package rate.
