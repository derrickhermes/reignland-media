# ReOC Explained: Why CASA's Remote Operator Certificate Matters for Your Drone Job

Introduction

If you're hiring a drone operator for commercial work—real estate photography, venue fly-throughs, or event coverage—you'll hear the term ReOC (Remote Operator's Certificate) mentioned. But what does it actually mean, and why should you care?

The answer determines whether your drone shoot is performed legally, safely, and with proper insurance coverage.

What Is a ReOC?

A Remote Operator's Certificate is the CASA-issued license that authorises a business to conduct commercial drone operations in Australia. It's not a pilot license—it's a company certificate that enables the business to:

- Legally charge for commercial drone services (including FPV, night ops, and aerial cinematography)

- Apply for specialised approvals (EVLOS, night ops, operations near people)

- Operate drones over 2kg commercially

- Maintain an approved Safety Management System (SMS)

ReOC holders are regulated aviation businesses. They file annual reports to CASA, maintain operational manuals, and undergo compliance audits.

ReOC vs. RePL — What's the Difference?

Certificate Who Holds It Purpose
**ReOC** (Remote Operator's Certificate) The business/company Authorises the business to conduct commercial drone operations
**RePL** (Remote Pilot's License) The individual pilot Authorises an individual to fly a drone for commercial purposes

A legitimate commercial operator has both: a ReOC for the business and RePL-holding pilots flying the actual missions.

Why the ReOC Matters to Clients

1. Insurance Validity

Most public liability insurers require the operator to hold a valid ReOC. If a claim arises from an unlicensed operation, the insurance policy can be voided—leaving you exposed.

2. Regulatory Compliance

Operators without a ReOC who charge for drone services are operating unlawfully under CASR Part 101. Penalties include fines and enforcement action from CASA.

3. Safety Standards

ReOC holders must maintain documented:

- Operational manuals

- Risk assessments

- Maintenance schedules

- Incident reporting procedures

- Pilot training records

This is the difference between a professional operator and someone with a consumer drone and a business card.

4. Access to Specialised Approvals

Want night filming? FPV fly-throughs? Operations near people? These require additional approvals that only ReOC holders can obtain.

The Australian Regulatory Framework

Commercial drone regulation in Australia operates under:

- CASR Part 101 — the primary regulatory framework for unmanned aircraft

- CASA Manual of Standards Part 101 — detailed operational standards

- AC 101-01 — advisory circular for ReOC applications

An operator without a ReOC charging for services is in breach of regulation 101.075 (operating a UAS for hire or reward without authorisation).

How to Verify a ReOC

Ask your operator for their ReOC number (format: CASA.ReOCA.XXXX). Then:

1. Check CASA's online register at casa.gov.au

2. Request a copy of their certificate — a legitimate operator provides it gladly

3. Cross-check the business name — the ReOC must match the trading entity

If the operator can't or won't provide their ReOC number—red flag.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I charge for drone work without a ReOC?

No. Charging for the commercial drone services covered in these guides—FPV cinematography, night work, and standard aerial production—requires a ReOC under CASA regulations.

What if I hire an unlicensed operator?

You may be party to an unlawful operation. If something goes wrong (property damage, injury), your insurance position could be compromised.

Is a ReOC hard to get?

The CASA application process is rigorous: documented safety case, operational manual, pilot qualifications, and a demonstrated safety culture. It separates professionals from hobbyists.

Does the ReOC expire?

Yes. ReOCs must be renewed periodically. Always verify the current status of an operator's certificate.
